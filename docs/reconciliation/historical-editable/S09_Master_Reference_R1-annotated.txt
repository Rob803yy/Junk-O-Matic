Master Engineering & Field Reference: Axial SCX6 Litterbuggy & Robotic Trash Picker
Document Purpose: Comprehensive offline technical manual, design log, hardware specification, and troubleshooting guide for the Axial SCX6-based Litterbuggy and autonomous/teleoperated robotic trash-collection system.
________________


1. System Architecture & Platform Overview
The Litterbuggy concept adapts heavy-duty 1/6-scale RC rock crawlers—specifically the Axial SCX6—into rugged, all-terrain utility vehicles capable of traversing parks, beaches, trails, and urban curbs to collect and haul litter. Adding a robotic trash-picking manipulator elevates the platform from a towed/pushed bin carrier into an active collection unit.
Core Subsystems
1. Mobility Base (Axial SCX6): Provides high-torque, four-wheel-drive locomotion, steep incline capability, and high payload capacity.
2. Payload & Containment System: A rear-mounted or towed hopper/bucket designed to hold collected debris without shifting the center of gravity (CoG) dangerously high.
3. Manipulator Arm & End-Effector: A multi-axis robotic arm or linear actuator mechanism fitted with a specialized gripper/claw capable of grasping irregular objects (cans, bottles, wrappers, cigarette butts).
4. Power Distribution & Dual-Battery Architecture: Isolation between high-draw traction motors/servos and sensitive logic/compute electronics.
5. Control & Telemetry: Long-range RC control (ELRS/Crossfire) paired with FPV video feed and optional companion computer (Raspberry Pi / Jetson) for computer vision and semi-autonomous picking.
________________


2. Base Platform: Axial SCX6 Technical Reference
The Axial SCX6 (Jeep JLU Wrangler or Honcho variants) is chosen over 1/10-scale crawlers (like the SCX10 or TRX-4) due to its massive structural rigidity, 1/5-scale steering hardware, and superior payload tolerance.
Key Stock Specifications & Limits
* Scale: 1/6
* Drivetrain: 4WD, 3-gear transmission with high/low two-speed mechanical shifting (operated via micro-servo).
* Axles: AR90 straight axles with metal gears and locked differentials (spools).
* Stock Motor/ESC: Spektrum Firma 120A Smart Brushless Crawler ESC + 1200Kv Sensored Brushless Motor.
* Stock Steering Servo: Spektrum S905 High-Torque 1/5-scale metal gear servo (approx. $38\text{ kg}\cdot\text{cm}$ to $40\text{ kg}\cdot\text{cm}$ at $6\text{V}$).
* Wheel Hex: $17\text{mm}$ heavy-duty hex hubs.
* Stock Weight: $\approx 11.3\text{ kg}$ ($25\text{ lbs}$) without batteries.
* Recommended Safe Continuous Payload: Up to $15\text{--}20\text{ kg}$ ($33\text{--}44\text{ lbs}$) additional cargo with proper suspension and cooling upgrades.
Critical SCX6 Upgrades for Litterbuggy Duty
1. Suspension Stiffening: Stock springs will sag immediately under a loaded trash bin and robotic arm.
   * Solution: Upgrade to heavier-rate springs (e.g., stiff progressive springs or custom linear springs), increase shock oil viscosity to $50\text{wt--}70\text{wt}$, and add preload spacers.
2. Steering Servo & BEC: Adding weight over the front axle (for the robotic arm) drastically increases scrubbing torque.
   * Solution: Upgrade to a direct-power $7.4\text{V}$ or $8.4\text{V}$ servo rated at $\ge 70\text{ kg}\cdot\text{cm}$ (e.g., AGFRC, Reefs RC, or NSDRC 1/5-scale servos) with an aluminum servo horn. Do not rely on the stock ESC's internal BEC for heavy steering + arm loads.
3. Tire Foams: Stock open-cell foams will fold and roll off the bead under heavy lateral loads.
   * Solution: Install dual-stage closed-cell/open-cell heavy-duty foams (e.g., Crawler Innovations Deuce's Wild or 3D-printed TPU airless inserts).
4. Transmission & Gearing: Always operate in Low Gear during trash collection operations to maximize torque, improve low-speed throttle resolution, and keep motor/ESC temperatures low. Consider dropping the pinion gear size by 2–3 teeth if running heavy payloads in warm weather.

3. Robotic Trash Picker & End-Effector Design
Designing a gripper for outdoor litter is uniquely challenging because targets vary wildly in geometry, rigidity, and weight—from flat paper wrappers and crushed aluminum cans to full glass bottles.
Kinematic Configurations
* Option A: 3-DOF or 4-DOF Articulated Arm (Front-Mounted)
   * Pros: Can reach into bushes, ditches, and around obstacles; looks and operates like a true mobile manipulator.
   * Cons: High moment arm stresses the front suspension; requires significant battery power and complex inverse kinematics (IK) or multi-channel mixing.
* Option B: Gantry / Linear Z-Axis Drop Picker
   * Pros: Simpler mechanics, lower power draw, extremely rigid, easier to automate with downward-facing computer vision.
   * Cons: Vehicle must drive directly over or immediately adjacent to the trash item.
* Option C: Sweeper / Paddle Drum (Continuous Collection)
   * Pros: Fast collection of small items (bottle caps, butts, cans) on flat terrain (beaches, sidewalks).
   * Cons: Struggles with tall grass, rocks, and large irregular debris.
End-Effector (Gripper) Best Practices
1. Compliant & High-Friction Fingertips: Rigid plastic or bare metal claws will drop glass and hard plastic bottles. Line the inner jaws with high-durometer silicone, EPDM rubber, or soft 3D-printed TPU pads (Shore 85A–95A) with ribbed tread patterns.
2. Pinch + Scoop Geometry: Design the lower jaw with a thin, beveled leading edge (like a dustpan lip) so it can slide underneath flat objects (cardboard, wrappers, flattened cans) against pavement or dirt.
3. Underactuated / Adaptive Fingers: Use linkage-driven or tendon-driven fingers that wrap around cylindrical objects (bottles/cans) rather than pinching at a single tangent point.
4. Actuation Choice:
   * High-Torque PWM Servos ($35\text{--}60\text{ kg}\cdot\text{cm}$): Easy to control, fast, built-in position feedback, but stall current can burn them out if jammed.
   * Linear Actuators (12V micro-actuators): Self-locking when unpowered (zero hold current), very high force, immune to shock loads, but slower cycle times.

4. Payload & Trash Containment Engineering
* Center of Gravity (CoG) Management: Keep the trash bin mounted as low as possible between the frame rails or directly behind the rear shock towers. Avoid tall bins that cause tip-overs on side-hills ( camber).
* Quick-Release Liner System: Design the rear hopper to accept standard 13-gallon or 30-gallon contractor trash bags secured via an elastic bungee loop or hinged clamping rim. Dumping should require zero tools.
* Front-to-Rear Weight Balance: Ideal static weight distribution is 55% Front / 45% Rear when empty, shifting to roughly 45% Front / 55% Rear when the bin is full. If the robotic arm is mounted on the front bumper, mount the battery banks toward the rear to counterbalance.

5. Electrical & Power Architecture
Golden Rule: Never power your receiver, flight controller, or companion computer from the same voltage rail that drives high-torque servos or traction motors without heavy filtering and isolation. Brownouts from servo stall currents will reboot your logic board mid-operation.
Recommended Dual-Bus Power System
   1. Traction Bus (3S or 4S LiPo, $11.1\text{V--}14.8\text{V}$, $5000\text{mAh--}10000\text{mAh}$):
   * Dedicated strictly to the Spektrum Firma 120A ESC and drive motor.
   * Keep standard EC5/IC5 connectors; inspect for heat after long runs.
   2. Auxiliary / Robotics Bus (3S or 4S LiPo, $5000\text{mAh}$, or Dedicated Step-Down Rail):
   * Servo Rail ($7.4\text{V}$ or $8.4\text{V}$): Powered by a dedicated high-current UBEC (e.g., Castle Creations 20A BEC Pro) capable of handling simultaneous stall spikes from the steering servo and robotic arm actuators.
   * Logic & Compute Rail ($5.1\text{V}$ Regulated): Isolated buck converter feeding the RC receiver, Raspberry Pi / Jetson, and cameras. Add a $1000\mu\text{F--}2200\mu\text{F}$ low-ESR electrolytic capacitor across the $5\text{V}$ rail to absorb transient voltage dips.
   * VTX / FPV Camera Rail ($9\text{V}$ or $12\text{V}$ Filtered): LC-filtered power source to eliminate motor/PWM noise lines in the video feed.
Wiring & Protection Checklist
   * Place a master inline blade fuse or resettable breaker ($30\text{A}$ for aux bus, $60\text{A--}80\text{A}$ for main traction) near each battery positive terminal.
   * Use braided PET expandable sleeving on all cables routed across the robotic arm joints to prevent chafing and snagging.
   * Leave a generous service loop at every pivot axis (shoulder, elbow, wrist, and steering linkage).

6. Control, FPV & Autonomy Stack
Radio Control (Teleoperation)
   * Protocol: ExpressLRS (ELRS) $915\text{MHz}$ or $2.4\text{GHz}$, or TBS Crossfire. Standard $2.4\text{GHz}$ surface radios often lose signal behind concrete walls, hills, or dense foliage at ground level.
   * Channel Mapping Strategy (12–16 Channels):
   * CH1: Steering
   * CH2: Throttle (with exponential curve softened around center for precision crawling)
   * CH3: 2-Speed Transmission (High/Low)
   * CH4: Arm Base Pan (Yaw)
   * CH5: Arm Shoulder (Pitch)
   * CH6: Arm Elbow (Pitch)
   * CH7: Wrist Roll / Pitch
   * CH8: Gripper Open/Close (assigned to a self-centering switch or rotary potentiometer with current/end-point limits)
   * CH9: Camera Gimbal / View Switcher (Drive Cam vs. Gripper Cam)
   * CH10: Auxiliary Lights / Beacon / Hopper Dump
Dual-Camera FPV Setup
Operating a robotic arm remotely without depth perception is difficult. Use a dual-camera video switcher:
   1. Navigation Camera (Wide FOV, $120^\circ\text{--}150^\circ$): Mounted high on the roll cage looking forward at a slight downward tilt () to see terrain hazards and spot litter at a distance.
   2. End-Effector / Gripper Camera (Narrow/Medium FOV, $70^\circ\text{--}90^\circ$): Mounted directly on the wrist or forearm looking straight between the gripper jaws for precision alignment during pickup.
Optional Computer Vision & Semi-Autonomy
      * Hardware: Raspberry Pi 5 (with Hailo AI HAT) or NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano running ROS 2 (Robot Operating System).
      * Perception Pipeline: Lightweight object detection model (YOLOv8n / YOLOv11n) trained on the TACO (Trash Annotations in Context) dataset to classify cans, bottles, plastic bags, and cups.
      * Visual Servoing: Rather than full autonomous navigation, implement "Assistive Visual Grab"—the operator drives the SCX6 near the trash, flips a switch, and the onboard computer uses the wrist camera to center the gripper over the detected bounding box and execute the grasp/deposit macro automatically.

7. Key Lessons Learned & Field Best Practices
      1. Ground-Level RF Attenuation is Severe: Antennas mounted low on an RC chassis lose range rapidly over wet grass and asphalt due to Fresnel zone interference. Mount the RC receiver and VTX antennas on a flexible mast at least $15\text{--}20\text{ cm}$ above the highest metal/carbon structural component.
      2. Servo Stall is the #1 Killer: If the gripper tries to close on a rock or curb, a digital servo will pull maximum stall current until its MOSFETs burn out.
      * Mitigation: Set strict mechanical end-points in your transmitter, integrate a mechanical spring-saver/compliant linkage in the gripper drive train, or monitor current via an inline shunt resistor to auto-cut power if current exceeds threshold for $>1.5\text{ seconds}$.
      3. Thermal Management at Low Speeds: Crawling slowly with a heavy load generates high current with almost zero natural airflow over the motor and ESC.
      * Mitigation: Install dual $30\text{mm}$ or $40\text{mm}$ high-RPM aluminum cooling fans directly onto the motor heatsink and ESC, wired to run continuously whenever the auxiliary bus is powered.
      4. Failsafe Configuration is Mandatory: A $35\text{ lb}$ SCX6 running away at full throttle is a serious safety hazard.
      * Mitigation: Program your receiver failsafe so that signal loss immediately sets Throttle to 0% (Neutral/Brake), Steering to Center, and freezes the Robotic Arm in its current position (do not command the arm to fold on failsafe, as it may crush an obstacle or itself).
      5. Ingress Protection (Dust, Sand, Wet Trash): Outdoor litter is frequently wet, sticky, or sandy. Use conformal coating (e.g., MG Chemicals 422B) on all exposed PCBs, pack axle bearings with marine-grade waterproof grease, and avoid exposed lead-screws near ground level where sand will bind the threads.

8. Offline Field Operations & Troubleshooting Checklists
Pre-Run Inspection Checklist
      * Verify main traction battery and auxiliary robotics battery voltages ($4.20\text{V/cell}$ full; never run below $3.50\text{V/cell}$ resting).
      * Confirm transmission is locked in Low Gear before loading payload.
      * Check wheel nuts ($17\text{mm}$ hex) and driveshaft set-screws (apply blue Loctite to all metal-to-metal fasteners).
      * Test radio failsafe by powering off transmitter while wheels are elevated off the ground.
      * Verify video feed clarity on both Navigation and Gripper cameras.
      * Check gripper silicone/TPU pads for tears, oil, or debris buildup.
Field Troubleshooting Matrix
Symptom
	Likely Cause
	Immediate Offline Fix
	Vehicle lurches or ESC cuts out when moving arm
	Voltage sag / brownout on shared BEC rail
	Isolate logic/receiver onto separate $5\text{V}$ BEC; reduce arm speed/acceleration rates in TX.
	Steering fails to turn when loaded on high-grip pavement
	Servo torque limit exceeded or servo horn stripped
	Verify BEC voltage is set to $7.4\text{V}/8.4\text{V}$; inspect splines on servo horn; avoid dry-steering while stationary.
	Motor or ESC thermal shutdown
	Gearing too tall for payload weight, or binding drivetrain
	Allow 15-min cooldown; check for grass/string wrapped around axle shafts; verify cooling fans are spinning.
	Arm jitters or oscillates uncontrollably
	Loose mechanical linkage, worn potentiometer, or insufficient BEC current
	Tighten all pivot bolts; check servo wiring for loose signal pins; add ferrite ring to long servo extension leads.
	Loss of video signal behind minor obstacles
	Low antenna placement or VTX overheating at low speed
	Elevate VTX antenna; lower VTX output power if sitting stationary for long periods without airflow.

9. Bill of Materials (BOM) & Spare Parts Field Kit
Always carry the following offline repair kit during field deployments:
      * Fasteners: Assorted M3 and M4 stainless steel socket-head cap screws, M4 locknuts, and medium-strength (Blue) threadlocker.
      * Drivetrain Spares: Spare $17\text{mm}$ wheel nuts, axle cross-pins, driveshaft rebuild kit, and spare pinion gear.
      * Electronics Spares: Spare high-torque servo (pre-centered), $30\text{A}$ blade fuses, XT60/EC5 adapters, zip ties, electrical tape, and heat-shrink tubing.
      * Tools: $1.5\text{mm}$, $2.0\text{mm}$, $2.5\text{mm}$, and $3.0\text{mm}$ hex drivers, $7\text{mm}$ & $17\text{mm}$ nut drivers, needle-nose pliers, flush cutters, and a compact LiPo cell checker.
